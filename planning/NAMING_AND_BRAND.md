# Naming and brand direction

**Status:** Provisional; the name is not approved as final. Trademark, domain, and social-handle availability have not been checked.

## Provisional recommendation

Use **FintaLab** as a replaceable working name. It has a football-specific point of view without sounding limited to one account type, and pairs naturally with player development. The proposed tagline is **“Turn every match into a next step.”** This communicates the report-to-development journey without promising a particular score or outcome.

Before final naming approval, conduct stakeholder pronunciation testing in Portuguese and target international markets, legal/trademark review, and domain/handle checks. Do not infer availability from this proposal.

## Brand personality and voice

- **Evidence-led:** distinguish observed information from interpretation.
- **Ambitious, not inflated:** encourage development without promising selection, transfers, or outcomes.
- **Competitive, not hostile:** support athlete progress and team decisions.
- **Clear:** explain what an evaluation can and cannot conclude.

Use concise, respectful language. Label fictional/demo data and simulations. Never frame missing evidence as poor performance.

## Creative direction

Combine original retro-football-game cues—crisp pixel accents, scoreboard numerals, pitch geometry—with editorial sports analytics. Keep the primary interface professional, legible, and calm. Use pixel art as a distinctive accent rather than a dense texture or interaction affordance.

### Logo concepts

- **Primary mark:** an original angular pitch/grid with a ball path and a single measurement node.
- **Horizontal lockup:** symbol plus FintaLab wordmark; keep the wordmark editable as text.
- **Secondary lockup:** centered stacked symbol and wordmark for narrow or square placements.
- **Icon-only:** grid-and-trajectory mark with no small lettering.
- **Variants:** dark-ink for light surfaces, light mark for dark surfaces, and monochrome for constrained reproduction.

The supplied original SVGs are starting assets, not a final trademark-cleared identity. Do not use third-party game marks, official card frames, or copied artwork.

### Logo usage guidelines

- Preserve clear space at least equal to one grid-cell width around the mark; never crowd it with interface content.
- Use the light lockup on dark green/dark canvas and the dark lockup on light surfaces. Avoid placing the green mark directly on busy imagery.
- Do not recolor the yellow/green trajectory independently, stretch the mark, add effects, or recreate the wordmark with pixel typography.
- Use the icon-only mark for small application/favorite icons; use a wordmark at sizes where it remains legible.
- Keep SVG edges crisp; do not blur or smooth pixel-art details. Provide accessible text when a logo conveys identity and empty alt text when it is purely decorative beside the same name.

WCAG contrast calculations for initial samples: `#F4F7F2` on `#101713` is 16.84:1; `#A7B5AA` on `#101713` is 8.52:1; white on `#168447` is 4.75:1; `#168447` on `#F5F7F2` is 4.40:1 (below 4.5:1 for normal text); `#FFD447` on `#101713` is 12.79:1. Prefer `brand.deep` for text/action on light backgrounds and recheck all final state combinations, font weights, and component boundaries before implementation.

## Typography and illustration

Use a highly legible sans-serif for body copy, forms, and navigation; a monospaced face for ratings and compact statistics; and a pixel display font only for short decorative headings. Use original pixel illustrations with consistent scale, crisp edges, restrained outlines, and a small shared palette. Provide non-pixel fallbacks for small screens and assistive technology.
